THIRD-PARTY ATTRIBUTION
-----------------------

This project includes shaders ported from Paper Shaders
(https://github.com/paper-design/shaders), Copyright Paper Design,
licensed under the Apache License, Version 2.0
(http://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0).

Ported shaders (Sources/Shaders/), 30 of the 76 shipped:

  Color Panels, Dithering, Dot Grid, Dot Orbit, Fluted Glass,
  Gem Smoke, God Rays, Grain Gradient, Halftone CMYK, Halftone Dots,
  Heatmap, Image Dithering, Lens Distortion, Liquid Metal, Mesh
  Gradient, Metaballs, Neuro Noise (original algorithm by @zozuar),
  Paper Texture, Perlin Noise,
  Pulsing Border, Simplex Noise, Smoke Ring, Spiral, Static Mesh
  Gradient, Static Radial Gradient, Swirl, Voronoi, Warp, Water, Waves.

Shared helpers in the shader prelude (hashes, value noise, simplex noise,
banding fix) are also derived from Paper Shaders.

The ports translate the original GLSL to Metal Shading Language and replace
texture-based randomizers with procedural hashes.

Several upstream shaders operate on a supplied image (u_image). Those ports
substitute a procedural source for the sampler, keeping the upstream
algorithm: Water, Fluted Glass, Halftone Dots, Halftone CMYK, Heatmap,
Image Dithering, Lens Distortion. Each
affected file documents its substitution in a header comment.

Heatmap additionally ports upstream's CPU pre-pass. Upstream does not hand
that shader a raw picture: `toProcessedHeatmap` box-blurs the source
silhouette at three radii and packs them into one RGB image first.
Sources/LerpCore/HeatmapData.swift is a translation of that routine — the
same integral-image box blur, the same multi-pass counts, the same radii as
fractions of the shape box, the same channel packing — run over a
procedurally drawn silhouette instead of a supplied file.

One upstream shader is deliberately not ported: Image Dithering. It shares
its pixel grid, its Bayer matrices and its threshold verbatim with upstream's
generative Dithering, which is already ported here; what it adds beyond that
is a multi-step luminance quantisation and an original-colours mode, some six
lines. Everything else that would make it look like a separate shader would
have to be an invented source image, so it belongs as parameters on Dithering
rather than as a shader of its own.

Aurora (Sources/Shaders/aurora.metal) is original work, not a port.

Game of Life (Sources/Shaders/game-of-life.metal,
Sources/LerpCore/LifeData.swift) is original work, not a port: upstream has no
cellular automaton. It implements Conway's B3/S23 rule (John Conway, 1970), the
Gosper glider gun (Bill Gosper), the R-pentomino, HighLife B36/S23 and its
12-cell replicator (Nathan Thompson), Maze B3/S12345, Day & Night
B3678/S34678, Seeds B2/S, and Life Without Death B3/S012345678. These rules and
cell coordinates are mathematical facts and not themselves copyrightable. The
continuous organism field, membrane lighting, bloom, fitted cameras, bounded
story epochs, sparse finite boards, trails, and solidity ramps are this
project's own.

Pipes (Sources/Shaders/pipes.metal, Sources/LerpCore/PipesData.swift) is
original work: a raymarched signed-distance-field rendering, sharing no code
with anything it references. It is an homage to Microsoft's 3D Pipes
screensaver (sspipes, Copyright (c) 1994-1995 Microsoft Corporation), and to
match that program's look it reuses published numeric constants from it:
lattice spacing 7 with pipe radius 1, ball joints of sqrt(2) x radius, a 90
degree field of view at a viewing distance of 75, a scene yaw step of
9.73156 degrees per reset, and its light position (90, 90, 150, 0) with
global ambient 1.0. The sixteen pipe materials are the OpenGL "teapot"
material table (emerald, jade, pearl, ruby, turquoise, brass, bronze,
copper, gold, silver, and assorted plastics and rubbers), a set of ambient,
diffuse, specular and shininess values long published in OpenGL sample code
and reference material. Facts and measurements are not themselves
copyrightable, but note the provenance if you redistribute.

Maze (Sources/Shaders/maze.metal, Sources/LerpCore/MazeData.swift) is
original work: an analytic ray caster over a procedurally carved maze, with
procedural brick, flagstone and board textures. It shares no code, textures
or artwork with anything it references. It is an homage to Microsoft's 3D
Maze screensaver (ssmaze, Copyright (c) Microsoft Corporation), and borrows
only its premise: a first-person, hand-on-the-wall walk through brick
corridors, with spinning polyhedra that turn the view upside down.


SHADERS ADDED NOVEMBER 2026
---------------------------

Original work, implementing published methods (algorithms, models and
published parameter values are not themselves copyrightable; no code from
the cited sources is used unless stated):

Reaction-Diffusion (Sources/Shaders/reaction-diffusion.metal,
Sources/LerpCore/ReactionDiffusionData.swift) simulates the Gray-Scott model
(P. Gray and S. K. Scott, 1983-84), implemented from Karl Sims'
Reaction-Diffusion Tutorial (https://www.karlsims.com/rd.html), using his
diffusion rates, 3x3 Laplacian weights, and coral and mitosis parameters. The
fingerprint and worm parameters are points from J. E. Pearson's map ("Complex
Patterns in a Simple System", Science 261, 1993) as charted by Robert Munafo.

Physarum (Sources/Shaders/physarum.metal, Sources/LerpCore/PhysarumData.swift)
implements Jeff Jones' agent model of Physarum polycephalum transport networks
(Artificial Life 16(2), 2010) in the synchronous form described by Sage Jenson
(https://cargocollective.com/sagejenson/physarum).

Ink Fluid (Sources/Shaders/ink-fluid.metal, Sources/LerpCore/InkFluidSolver.swift,
Sources/LerpCore/InkFluidData.swift) implements Jos Stam's stable-fluids method
("Real-Time Fluid Dynamics for Games", GDC 2003) with vorticity confinement
from Fedkiw, Stam and Jensen, "Visual Simulation of Smoke" (SIGGRAPH 2001).

Curl Flow (Sources/Shaders/curl-flow.metal) implements Bridson, Hourihan and
Nordenstam, "Curl-Noise for Procedural Fluid Flow" (SIGGRAPH 2007). Its look
is inspired by Tyler Hobbs' essay "Flow Fields" (2020). The potential is the
prelude's simplex noise (Paper Shaders, Apache-2.0, above).

Dusk Terrain (Sources/Shaders/dusk-terrain.metal) implements techniques
described in Inigo Quilez's articles (https://iquilezles.org/articles): value
noise with analytic derivatives, heightfield raymarching, penumbra soft
shadows, analytic fog and cosine palettes. No code from those articles or any
Shadertoy shader is included. Its lattice hash applies the first round of
Chris Wellons' "lowbias32" integer mix (hash-prospector, public domain /
Unlicense).

Agate (Sources/Shaders/agate.metal) was written from The Book of Shaders by
Patricio Gonzalez Vivo and Jen Lowe, chapters "Cellular Noise"
(https://thebookofshaders.com/12/) and "Fractal Brownian Motion"
(https://thebookofshaders.com/13/). None of the book's example code is copied.

Sandpile (Sources/Shaders/sandpile.metal, Sources/LerpCore/SandpileData.swift,
Sources/LerpCore/GPUSimulation.swift) implements the Abelian sandpile model of
Bak, Tang and Wiesenfeld (1987), stepped on the GPU.

Cutout Shadows (Sources/Shaders/cutout-shadows.metal) adapts code from Ronja
Böhringer's shader tutorials (https://www.ronja-tutorials.com, source at
https://github.com/ronja-tutorials/ShaderTutorials), licensed under Creative
Commons Attribution 4.0 International
(https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Adapted, translated from HLSL
to MSL and modified: the round union from "2D SDF Combination", the
soft-shadow march from "2D SDF Shadows", radial repetition from "2D SDF Space
Manipulation", the rectangle distance from "2D SDF Basics", and the threshold
dither from "Dithering". Its interleaved-gradient-noise dither follows Jorge
Jimenez (SIGGRAPH 2014) and its R2 dither follows Martin Roberts (2018).

Ports (each file header carries the full upstream notice):

Star Nest (Sources/Shaders/star-nest.metal) is ported from "Star Nest" by
Pablo Roman Andrioli (Kali), https://www.shadertoy.com/view/XlfGRj,
Copyright (c) 2013 Pablo Roman Andrioli, licensed under the MIT License.

Topologica (Sources/Shaders/topologica.metal) is ported from "Topologica" by
Otavio Good, https://www.shadertoy.com/view/4djXzz, dedicated to the public
domain under CC0 1.0. The port (its Classic form) changes the march step
count, lattice hash and glow gain. The same file holds this project's
remixes of it as further forms; see "Topologica remixes" below.

Sea and Moon (Sources/Shaders/sea-and-moon.metal) is ported from "Sea and
moon" by mrange (Mårten Rånge), https://www.shadertoy.com/view/csfXzN,
dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0. Upstream's tanh_approx helper,
marked "License: Unknown", is not included.

Halo (Sources/Shaders/halo.metal) is a modified port of "Halo" by Xor
(@XorDev), https://fragcoord.xyz/s/hp79nubv, Copyright (c) 2026 @Xor,
licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. Translated from
FragCoord's golf dialect to Metal, with time slowed and seeded, a speed
parameter added, and short-axis scaling. Not endorsed by the author.

The Cathedral, Coral and Lattice forms of Fractal Multiverse
(Sources/Shaders/fractal-multiverse.metal, forms 1-3) are original work written in the idiom of Yohei
Nishitsuji's "fractal, the multiverse" (https://fragcoord.xyz/s/ftisi70e, MIT;
ported here as Fractal Multiverse): a short fixed march through a slab of
fractal space, lit by each sample's fold orbit. No code from that shader is
reused. They implement published methods: Tom Lowe's Mandelbox (2010); the
sphere-inversion Apollonian packing as described by Inigo Quilez; Knighty's
kaleidoscopic IFS octahedral fold (2010) and Kali's (Pablo Roman Andrioli)
|p|/p.p - c inversion (the Kaliset, 2010), combined in the Lattice form with
a four-dimensional double rotation; and orbit-trap colouring.

Castle To Castle (Sources/Shaders/castle-to-castle.metal) and Fractal D
(Sources/Shaders/fractal-d.metal) are ports of "castle to castle -r6-"
(https://fragcoord.xyz/s/cltuv8os) and "fractal -d-"
(https://fragcoord.xyz/s/fo2g251b) by Yohei Nishitsuji (@YoheiNishitsuji),
Copyright (c) 2026 @YoheiNishitsuji, licensed under the MIT License. Castle To
Castle marches 20 longer steps by default (100 upstream) and adds a tint;
Fractal D marches 24 by default (100 upstream), uses a polynomial atan2, and
slowly drifts upstream's fixed ring offset. The "Upstream" preset of each
restores the original step count.

Torque (Sources/Shaders/torque.metal) and Axes (Sources/Shaders/axes.metal)
are modified ports of "Torque" (https://fragcoord.xyz/s/t4e02wdx) and "Axes"
(https://fragcoord.xyz/s/a340ofb0) by Xor (@XorDev), Copyright (c) 2026 @Xor,
licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. Translated
from FragCoord's golf dialect to Metal, with time slowed and seeded, speed
(and, for Torque, drift) parameters added, and short-axis scaling. Not
endorsed by the author.

Fractal Multiverse (Sources/Shaders/fractal-multiverse.metal, form 0) and Simulated
Reality (Sources/Shaders/simulated-reality.metal) are ports of "fractal, the
multiverse" (https://fragcoord.xyz/s/ftisi70e) and "simulated reality -w-"
(https://fragcoord.xyz/s/sa07o47u) by Yohei Nishitsuji (@YoheiNishitsuji),
Copyright (c) 2026 @YoheiNishitsuji, licensed under the MIT License. Simulated
Reality caps its march at 64 steps by default (89 upstream, available as the
"Upstream" preset) and replaces the mouse-driven sun colour with a parameter.

Pulse Rings (Sources/Shaders/pulse-rings.metal) and String Art
(Sources/Shaders/string-art.metal, Sources/LerpCore/VertexShaderArtData.swift)
are ported from "pulse" (https://www.vertexshaderart.com/art/JnRaHZcXYv7WBhq5b)
and "stringart" (https://www.vertexshaderart.com/art/EgLk7JDok4GhxPeMH) by gman
(Gregg Tavares), dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0: "Any art by
"gman" is CC0" (https://github.com/greggman/vertexshaderart.com, LICENSE.md).
The originals are WebGL vertex shaders; the ports re-express them as a
per-pixel inverse evaluation and as a CPU evaluation binned into screen tiles.

Fractal and 4D geometry (original work implementing published mathematics;
no code from the cited articles or from any Shadertoy shader is included):

Menger Sponge (Sources/Shaders/menger-sponge.metal) renders the Menger sponge
(Karl Menger, 1926) and the Jerusalem cube (Eric Baird, 2011), with distance
estimation by folding space as described in Inigo Quilez's "Menger Fractal"
article and Mikael Hvidtfeldt Christensen's (Syntopia) "Distance Estimated 3D
Fractals" series, including Knighty's kaleidoscopic IFS.

Fractal Pyramids (Sources/Shaders/sierpinski-pyramid.metal) was written from
Knighty's kaleidoscopic IFS (fractalforums.com, 2010) and Christensen's
"Distance Estimated 3D Fractals (III): Folding Space". Its icosahedral fold
was derived for this project from the H3 reflection group. Its fly-through
uses over-relaxed sphere tracing after Keinert et al., "Enhanced Sphere
Tracing" (STAG 2014), along camera routes computed offline for this project.

Mandelbulb (Sources/Shaders/mandelbulb.metal) implements the Mandelbulb
formula of Daniel White and Paul Nylander (2009) with the running-derivative
distance estimator of Hart, Sandin and Kauffman ("Ray tracing deterministic
3-D fractals", SIGGRAPH 1989), as explained by Inigo Quilez and in
Christensen's "(V): The Mandelbulb", with over-relaxed sphere tracing from
Keinert et al., "Enhanced Sphere Tracing" (2014).

Mandelbox (Sources/Shaders/mandelbox.metal) renders Tom Lowe's Mandelbox
(fractalforums.com, 2010) with the scalar running-derivative distance
estimate as explained in Christensen's "(VI): The Mandelbox", marched with
Keinert et al.'s over-relaxed sphere tracing. Its camera routes were computed
offline for this project.

Quaternion Julia (Sources/Shaders/quaternion-julia.metal) raymarches 3D
slices of 4D quaternion Julia sets (q^2 + c and q^3 + c) with the distance
estimator of Hart, Sandin and Kauffman (1989), slicing and morphing the set as
described in Inigo Quilez's article "3D Julia sets".

Kleinian (Sources/Shaders/kleinian.metal) implements the sphere-inversion
Apollonian construction described by Inigo Quilez; Knighty's pseudo-Kleinian
fractal (fractalforums.com, 2011); the Maskit-slice Kleinian group from
Mumford, Series and Wright, "Indra's Pearls" (2002), extended to three
dimensions after Jos Leys' renderings; and Keinert et al.'s over-relaxed
sphere tracing.

Polytope 4D (Sources/Shaders/polytope-4d.metal,
Sources/LerpCore/Polytope4DData.swift) renders the regular convex 4-polytopes
(8-, 16-, 24-, 120- and 600-cell) and p,q-duoprisms from their standard vertex
coordinates (the 24 Hurwitz units, the 120 unit icosians, and the classical
600-vertex set of the 120-cell) under a double rotation, with perspective or
stereographic projection from S^3.

Topologica remixes (forms 1-9 of Sources/Shaders/topologica.metal, each a
remix of "Topologica" by Otavio Good, https://www.shadertoy.com/view/4djXzz,
dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0; each keeps upstream's orbiting
volumetric glow and colouring and replaces its noise lattice):

Lattice, Filigree and Jacks: a kaleidoscopic-IFS wire network written from
Knighty's technique as described by Christensen, with a seamless
self-similar zoom.

Minimal surfaces and Strata: blended triply periodic minimal surfaces
(gyroid, Schwarz P, Schwarz D, Neovius), drawing the curves where two of them
intersect, integrated exactly along each march step.

Tesseract, 24-cell, Hopf fibration and Torus knots: sliced tesseractic and
24-cell honeycombs, the Hopf fibration and torus knots in S^3, with
closed-form glow integration.
